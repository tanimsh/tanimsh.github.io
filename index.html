Recent projects

2024–2026

Geospatial & ML-Guided Emergency Response

A modular workflow integrating geospatial analysis and machine learning for equitable, evidence-based placement of mobile health services.

This work examines how mobile health services can be positioned to reach underserved populations. It surveys prior practice, outlines gaps in current planning, and proposes a general decision-support approach grounded in widely used methods. Geospatial components include mapping of populations and service venues, network-based accessibility measures, and spatial optimization to compare alternative configurations. Machine learning elements support pattern detection, demand estimation, and model validation, with attention to avoiding overfitting and ensuring transportability. The result is a scalable planning workflow for mobile health services across varied settings.

2023–2025

Matrix-Based Resilience Assessment

A widely applicable metric set, demonstrated on community water supply, for assessing resilience to cascading infrastructure failures.

With rising climate threats and cascading impacts from infrastructure failure, there is a growing need to strengthen community resilience. This work extends a resilience matrix framework adaptable for communities, utility providers, and government agencies, establishing a set of widely applicable metrics that users can tailor to their organization. To test the approach, it focuses on a single critical community function — the ability to supply water — culminating in a case study assessing water-supply resilience within and around a county in the southeastern US, revealing strengths and weaknesses in responding to cascading disruptions.

2023–2025

Hydropower Dam & Reservoir Systems

Causal-loop modeling traces infrastructure-to-society dependencies for dam systems, distinguishing immediate from long-term impacts.

An approach to understand how dam and reservoir systems function and interact under natural and man-made hazards using causal loop diagrams. It maps direct links among infrastructure sectors and connects them to social dimensions — safety, health, economy, cohesion, governance — while separating near-term disruption from longer-term degradation and recovery. Simple visuals summarize where dependencies concentrate, and the analysis notes gaps in current sector coverage to prioritize mitigation and strengthen resilience.

2025–2026

Integrated Modeling of Tick Risk

Multi-year field surveillance combined with environmental context and cautious predictive modeling to characterize broad tick-risk patterns.

This work examines seasonality and environmental context for a prevalent tick species using multi-year surveillance from state parks and historic sites, paired with statistical and spatial modeling. It considers widely available environmental features — land cover, vegetation indices, topography, and climate — and uses standard validation to assess predictive performance. Mapping and cross-validation are applied conservatively to support planning and communication, providing a restrained, decision-support overview of potential risk patterns for Amblyomma americanum in South Carolina.

2023–2024

Cross-Phase Criticality Under Floods

GIS shortest-path rerouting and betweenness centrality reveal how road-segment importance shifts across normal, response, and recovery phases.

The study couples time-based shortest paths (ArcGIS Pro) with network betweenness to map rerouting and link importance before, during, and after a flood. Local and secondary roads are frequently cut, forcing flows onto higher-capacity corridors; added travel time and barrier locations expose vulnerable segments. Consistently high-criticality routes (I-85 and key state/federal highways) offer clear targets for mitigation and rapid restoration. Results synthesize multiple trip purposes — hospitals, work, schools, goods, transit — to guide phase-aware prioritization and resilience investment.

2025–2026

Hesitancy Signals & Vaccination Gaps

A GIS-integrated workflow links county-level hesitancy signals with vaccination coverage to map patterns and test associations.

Vaccination is a core public-health safeguard, yet uptake varies across communities, and official coverage statistics can lag and mask local disparities. This work pairs county-level indicators of vaccine hesitancy from social-media activity with official vaccination coverage to create a statewide picture of sentiment versus uptake. The comparison highlights counties where skepticism and protection diverge, and counties that outperform expectations — helping target communications, mobile services, and partnerships, and supporting equitable, privacy-conscious resource allocation.

2023–2025

Mapping Interdependent Critical Infrastructure

A structured approach to define infrastructure functions and trace sector-to-sector and sector-to-society interdependencies.

This work develops a concise, hazard-agnostic framework for understanding how critical infrastructures function and interact under natural and man-made hazards. Using standardized sector definitions and publicly available guidance, it replaces ad-hoc judgments with a clearer basis for assessing interdependencies. The analysis considers both direct effects between sectors and broader links to social systems — safety, health, economy, cohesion, governance — while separating near-term disruption from longer-term degradation and recovery, and recommends consistent documentation to support resilience prioritization.

2018–2021

Meta-Analysis of Evacuation Predictors

A scale-aware meta-analysis pools odds-ratio effects into robust, generalizable coefficients for key evacuation predictors.

To reconcile inconsistent findings across prior studies, this synthesis standardizes predictors and pools effects with random-effects models and prediction intervals, ranking the relative influence of each factor and quantifying between-study heterogeneity. The measurement-conscious approach enables apples-to-apples comparisons and stable effect sizes. Moderator tests reveal when and where effects shift. These pooled coefficients feed the neighborhood-level prediction model, replacing ad-hoc parameters with evidence-based values.

2024–2026

Transformer-Based Outbreak Forecasting

Fusing geospatial, clinical, mobility, and social signals with a spatiotemporal transformer to forecast flu, COVID, and RSV hotspots.

A transformer-based framework forecasts infectious-disease hotspots across flu, COVID, and RSV. A spatiotemporal transformer ingests geospatial features, mobility, environmental context, healthcare utilization, and privacy-preserving social-media signals, using attention to weight relationships across places, populations, and time. It produces probabilistic risk maps and ranked ZIP-level alerts with lead-time estimates to guide testing, vaccination, and staffing. Reliability is supported by cross-validated backtesting, calibration, uncertainty quantification, and drift monitoring; fairness through bias audits and interpretable attention summaries.

2017–2022 · AAG Best Paper

Hurricane Evacuation Modeling

Block-group-level predictions of who evacuates and who stays, so agencies can target support where it is least likely to occur.

This project predicts household evacuation at census block-group resolution by integrating multivariate socio-demographic and housing indicators to estimate who evacuates and who remains. Mapping decisions against neighborhood characteristics reveals where evacuation is least likely and where tailored communication, transport assistance, and shelter services are most needed. Moving beyond coarse county-level averages, the model captures neighborhood heterogeneity that shapes real behavior. Recognized with the AAG 2021 Best Paper award, the work advances equity-focused evacuation planning.

2018–2022

Optimizing Evacuation Routes

Closest-facility routing and suitability scoring pinpoint exit assignments and bottlenecks to guide traffic operations.

Using block-group demand estimates, each neighborhood is assigned to the most suitable exit via closest-facility analysis, then corridors are evaluated for performance and bottleneck probability. Route-suitability scores blend roadway characteristics, population served, and access constraints to prioritize improvements. Outputs guide contraflow, signal timing, signage, staging of tow trucks and EMS, and identification of critical infrastructure needs — a practical, GIS-driven playbook that converts behavioral forecasts into actionable traffic and resource decisions.

2017–2022

Demographics & Evacuation Decisions

Evacuation is best explained by combined socio-demographic profiles; single attributes can mislead despite large mean effects.

This study quantifies how socio-demographic factors relate to evacuation and compares their separability across populations. Meta-analytic estimates suggest race carries a large average effect size, but distribution plots show substantial overlap — evidence that race alone does not deterministically predict behavior. Composite profiles that include housing tenure, income, age, household structure, prior experience, and orders better explain decisions, pointing to community-specific outreach that addresses transportation, caregiving, and risk perception rather than single-attribute targeting.

2018–2019

Vulnerability Identification Tools

A configurable GIS tool scores evacuation vulnerability from census-block indicators to surface where support matters most.

An interactive workflow ingests block-level census data and user-selected socioeconomic factors — income, tenure, age, disability, vehicle access — to produce vulnerability scores and maps by county. Users can weight variables, filter to specific communities, and export ranked tables. Optional hazard overlays reveal where social vulnerability and exposure coincide, guiding transportation assistance, shelter siting, and accessible communication. The tool is modular and repeatable statewide, enabling agencies to plan resources where evacuation is least feasible.

2019–2020

Heat Mitigation via Tree Shade

Hillshade-based modeling shows morning tree shade cools land-surface temperatures, informing targeted urban greening.

Canopy and height data were combined with solar position to compute hourly shade indices, linked to Landsat land-surface temperature across seasons for Tampa and New York City. Mixed-effects estimates showed consistent morning cooling where shade intersects paved surfaces and building canyons, with larger effects in Tampa. Scenario maps identify where added canopy yields the biggest thermal benefit — schoolyards, transit stops, pedestrian corridors, and heat-vulnerable neighborhoods — offering actionable planting priorities for resilience plans.

Other Projects

2021–2022

Resilience of Transport Infrastructure

A decision tool ranks road segments by combined criticality and hazard exposure to target high-value, at-risk investments.

For FDOT, a geospatial assessment scores each road segment on two axes: criticality (functional class, AADT, redundancy, access to hospitals/shelters, freight value) and exposure (flood depth/surge, wind, scour, sea-level rise). Users can adjust weights, run scenarios, and visualize results via bivariate maps. Outputs include segment rankings and treatment shortlists that link assets to hazards and consequences of failure — supporting grant applications and capital programming so limited funds protect the segments whose disruption would most impair mobility and emergency response.

2021–2022

Dynamic Site Selection Tools

A modular Python–GIS engine screens parcels with multi-criteria scoring to recommend top sites with explainable rankings.

The workflow ingests zoning, parcels, utilities, environmental constraints, access to roads/markets, and operational criteria, normalizes each layer, and applies weighted linear combination (or AHP) to produce suitability surfaces. Users tailor weights to objectives and run quick what-if scenarios. Results return a sortable list and map of candidates; the highest-scoring locations are highlighted with factor-by-factor explanations and sensitivity analysis. The tool is portable to any region given comparable layers, enabling defensible, data-driven decisions for acquisitions, permitting, and phased development.

2021–2022

Utility System Vulnerability

A multi-criteria GIS model maps septic-failure risk where flooding, soils, groundwater, and density converge.

Elevation, flood zones, soil permeability, groundwater depth, and observed nutrient/bacteria data were integrated with system age and density to build a composite vulnerability surface. Hotspots mark where inundation and poor attenuation coincide near waterways or dense clusters of on-lot systems. Results are summarized by zone with population counts, potential load, and downstream receptors, producing a prioritized action list — sewer extensions, inspections, backup power for pumps, and targeted outreach. The workflow is repeatable and auditable, directing limited funds to the highest-risk locations.

2019–2020

Optimal Transit Station Siting

Suitability and service-area modeling identifies rail-station candidates that maximize population and job access.

In Hillsborough County, sites were screened using land use, urban density, and vacant-land availability to find parcels accommodating station facilities and 100+ parking spaces. Network Analyst generated walk/drive service areas to estimate reachable population and employment, revealing coverage, overlap, and spacing trade-offs along the corridor. A shortlist matrix summarizes each candidate's feasibility and catchment, supporting corridor alternatives analysis and stakeholder engagement with transparent criteria, clear maps, and quantitative comparisons.

2017–2019

Florida Crash Location Analysis

Statewide crash mapping, density surfaces, and network distances reveal spatial patterns in severity and exposure.

Using scripted geoprocessing, point feature classes were created for all reported crashes, with kernel-density maps highlighting hotspots and shortest-path road distances computed from each crash to the driver's home. Summary graphics explored relationships by severity, roadway class, lighting, and urban–rural context. Generalized models tested whether proximity to home predicted severity after controlling for confounders; distance was not significant, suggesting local context and roadway environment dominate. The pipeline provides an auditable method to prioritize enforcement and engineering countermeasures.

2016–2017

CNG Conversion Impacts

Environmental gains from CNG adoption are quantified alongside rebound effects from cheaper fuel and induced travel.

The study assembles vehicle registrations, fuel prices, and mode shares to estimate conversion from gasoline to CNG and projects emissions using standard factors (PM2.5, NOx, CO2). A demand module captures elastic responses to lower operating costs that can increase vehicle kilometers traveled, partially offsetting tailpipe benefits. Scenarios test pricing, congestion charges, and transit incentives. Findings emphasize that technology shifts must pair with demand management and fleet policies to achieve durable improvements in congestion and air quality.
